Scholarly transparency

How to interpret the data

Different evidence levels

Not every numerical value means the same thing. The underlying projects supply three distinct forms of evidence:

  • Curated records: Early Yiddish and Middle High German include annotated occurrence records, subject to further philological checks.
  • Published book totals: Old English frequencies originate from published analyses. Several expression labels share a group frequency, so values must not be added across rows.
  • Search candidates: Middle English and Old Norse contain automatically retrieved lexical matches, not validated emotional attestations.

Do not pool raw frequencies across the five languages. Corpus sizes, annotation methods and counting units differ.

FAIR orientation and reuse

This release links the five traditions through stable local identifiers and machine-readable vocabularies. Publication-level persistent identifiers and the new compilation's reuse license are not yet approved.

  • Structured expression metadata and source-domain labels
  • Ten controlled analytical emotion categories
  • Attribution to the original corpus providers
  • Source text and copyrighted passages excluded
  • Research provenance and explicit limitations

The site is a prepublication preview, not a claim that the underlying philological classifications are fully validated.
